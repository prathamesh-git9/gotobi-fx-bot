# Frozen rules and test protocol

Written before computing a strategy return on this dataset. Eight hypotheses below. Fixed constants are conventions from the cited papers where specified or simple ex ante definitions; no holdout tuning. All source timestamps are UTC, signals use completed bars and entries use the **next bar open**. Mid=(bid+ask)/2 defines signals. All long fills are ask in/bid out; all shorts bid in/ask out. A stop and target touched in one bar resolve at the stop. If the next open gaps through a stop, fill at that worse open. No commissions beyond Standard-feed spread, no swap because positions close intraday. No extra slippage is available in M1 bars, so estimates remain optimistic around news.

Each rule has at most one open position **across its symbols**. When signals collide, fixed symbol order XAUUSD, USTEC, US500, EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY breaks ties. Cross-rule portfolios are **not** simulated. If the feed has no bar at a scheduled exit, close at the last available same-session bar close before the gap; if an unexpected gap occurs earlier, close at the pre-gap bar close. For 2%-risk compounding, each trade sizes exposure to lose 2% at its stated initial stop, with no leverage/margin cap; results may therefore be economically unattainable, particularly for tiny stops. Negative balance is floored at zero. Main inference is mean net R, not final balance. Random-direction control uses the exact signal/entry times and stop distances, with independent seeded ± direction; it is subjected to the same exit logic. No free parameter was re-tuned after 2021-12-31. Training: data through 2021-12-31; holdout: 2022-01-01 through 2026-09-30. Report full sample from 2016-10-01 (indices from 2019-08) and recent 2024-10-01–2026-09-30. One-sided t tests on mean R versus zero and paired rule-minus-random R, Bonferroni by 8. Serial dependence makes these p values exploratory. **Audit note:** The original text applied the one-position constraint within each symbol; after viewing the first holdout output, we corrected it to the task's cross-symbol requirement. Gap handling was likewise corrected after the first pass. These were implementation corrections rather than return-based parameter choices, but the holdout is no longer a strict one-look holdout; treat inference as exploratory.

1. **SMC sweep → displacement → FVG retest (R1).** XAUUSD, EURUSD, GBPUSD, USTEC, US500; M15; UTC 07:00–19:45. A bullish sweep bar's low undercuts the lowest low of the previous 20 *completed* M15 bars and closes back above it; bearish is symmetric. In the next 1–2 M15 bars a displacement candle has body ≥ 1.2 × median body of the prior 20 bars, closes in the sweep direction, and makes a three-candle FVG: bullish low > high two bars earlier, bearish high < low two bars earlier. The first of the next 1–4 bars to touch the FVG midpoint and close on the displacement side triggers entry next open in displacement direction. Initial stop beyond sweep extreme by one spread; target 2R; otherwise exit at 20:45 UTC M15 open. Only one setup/trade at a time. This *adds* FVG confirmation to previously failed plain sweeps.
2. **Support/resistance break and retest (R2).** Same five symbols; M15; UTC 07:00–19:45. Resistance/support is max high/min low of previous 20 completed M15 bars. A close beyond it by ≥ 0.1 × prior 20-bar median range sets a breakout side. Within next 1–4 bars, a bar touches the old level and closes back on breakout side; enter next open. Stop on opposite side of retest candle plus one spread; target 2R; otherwise 20:45 UTC M15 open. A fresh breakout invalidates older setups; no overlap. This tests both breakouts and breakdowns with a retest, not a standalone range fade.
3. **5-minute US opening-range breakout (R3).** USTEC CFD; exchange session 09:30–16:00 America/New_York; M5. First 09:30–09:35 mid candle determines direction from open to close; doji skips. Its high/low is the level. First subsequent M5 close beyond that level on the selected side triggers next-open entry. Stop 10% of previous 14 completed NY-session days' true-range ATR from actual entry; no profit target; exit at 15:55 M5 open. At most one trade/day. Signal adaptation of Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz (2024): their stop-entry order is replaced by next-open execution per task requirement; their stock universe, share-volume and 4× cap cannot be replicated with one CFD.
4. **ORB with relative tick activity (R4).** R3 with first-five-minute quote count `n` ≥ mean first-five-minute `n` on previous 14 sessions. Same stop/exit. This is a single-index *activity filter*, not the paper's top-20 stocks-in-play portfolio. No stock selection or real share volume exists in these files.
5. **SPX noise band + quote-weighted VWAP (R5).** US500 CFD, NY session M30, decisions at completed HH:00/HH:30 bars starting 10:00. At each bar-end time, σ is mean absolute (mid close / NY-session mid open − 1) over the previous 14 sessions at that time. Upper=max(today open, previous session close)×(1+σ); lower=min(open, previous close)×(1−σ). Long if close > upper, short if < lower, entry next M5 open. While held, a completed M30 close crossing max(upper, today's cumulative `n`-weighted typical-mid VWAP) for long or min(lower,VWAP) for short exits next M5 open; opposite breakout may enter only after exit and next signal. Initial catastrophe stop 1× prior 14-day ATR (needed for R sizing); no profit target; exit by 15:55. Published bands and VWAP exit preserved, but `n`-weighted CFD quote VWAP is not exchange VWAP.
6. **First/last half-hour momentum (R6).** US500 CFD, NY M30. Compare 10:00 mid close to previous NY-session 16:00 mid close; positive => long, negative => short (zero skips). Entry at 15:30 M5 open (the next bar after the 15:25–15:30 M5 decision confirming the known morning direction); exit 15:55 M5 open. Catastrophe stop 1× previous 14-session ATR; no profit target. This is a tradable sign implementation of Gao et al. (2018), whose primary published result is a predictive regression, not this exact stop/position sizing.
7. **Round-number crossing continuation (R7).** EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY, M5; UTC 07:00–19:45. EUR/GBP grid 0.0050; JPY grid 0.50. When a completed M5 close crosses the nearest grid level from one side to the other and the previous M5 close was on the old side, enter next open with crossing direction. Same test on placebo grid shifted by 0.0025/0.25; retain separate real/placebo diagnostics. Stop 0.25 grid units, target 1 grid unit, time exit after 30 minutes or at 20:55 UTC M5 open. The 15-minute post-cross behavior in Osler (2005) is also measured as a price diagnostic.
8. **Round-number approach fade (R8).** Same three FX pairs and M5 grid/placebo. First bar of a local approach whose M5 high/low touches a grid level while its close remains on the approach side triggers next-open entry *away* from that level. Require previous M5 close at least 0.25 grid units from the level and bar close within 0.10 grid units of it. Stop 0.25 grid units beyond level, target 0.5 grid units away, time exit after 30 minutes or at 20:55 UTC. This probes the take-profit clustering/reversal hypothesis in Osler (2003), not a claim to observe stops.

For R3/R4, tick-confirmation diagnostics also subset entries by 09:30–09:35 `(up−dn)/(up+dn)` matching the ORB direction and compare with unfiltered results; R2 uses the signal bar's quote-direction proxy and `n` exceeding the previous 20-bar median as a second diagnostic. These are exploratory subset comparisons, not extra tuned rules and no result is called real order flow.
